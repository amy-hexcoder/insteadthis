It's no secret that dogs are our best friends, and watching your puppy do all the cute things is a [true delight](/stories/paw-parents-travel-checklist-while-road-tripping-with-their-fur-babies)! But how much do we genuinely know about them? Many myths and misconceptions have been circulated for decades about dogs, including their eating habits, behavior, and nutritional needs.
Perhaps you have noticed them leaning up against you as you walk him. There may be times when they attack the vacuum cleaner or roll around in smelly things. These [behaviors](https://www.vitalessentialsraw.com/blog/9-weird-things-dogs-do-and-why-they-do-that-behavior/) might leave you wondering why my dog is acting this way. Take a look at these dog behavior myths to know your pet better.

## • TAIL WAGGING

A dog's tail is one of the most prominent indications of its mood since dogs express themselves physically. If a dog wags its tail, it may not always be out of friendliness. Generally, a big, swoopy wag indicates that a dog is happy, but other types of wags may indicate nervousness or aggression.
An erect tail with wags is a sign of excitement, which indicates unpredictable behavior. Let's say your dog runs up to the door whenever someone knocks. Furthermore, a stiff, no-wagging tail can be a warning sign of aggressive behavior. It's important to know what situations trigger each type of tail wag.

## • DOGS LOOK GUILTY WHEN THEY MISBEHAVE

Imagine a messy scene with feathers floating in the air, pillows discarded, and dried spittle stains on the carpet. Amidst all this chaos, your dog is sitting with its paws tucked under its head, eyes drooping, saying, “Mommy, I'm sorry!”
It is assumed that they are guilty because of their past behavior. In actuality, your pet's guilt is only a reflection of your behavior. Dogs are not capable of reflecting on past behavior. When they sense your unhappiness, they become guilty, too.

## • DOGS LIKE TO LAZE AROUND MOST OF THE TIME

If your dog sleeps a lot or lays around all day, it is not because they are bored! Chihuahuas spend half of their lives sleeping! It is quite common for dogs to spend most of their day relaxing, but they should also be active every day.
A bored dog will often exhibit destructive behavior, such as tearing furniture apart, scratching, and pacing.

## • A SICK DOG ONLY EATS GRASS

Don't panic if you see your dog munching on some grass! All sorts of dogs eat grass, so it is perfectly normal. Wild dogs, like wolves, are also guided by this instinct.
Dogs consume grass for many reasons, including fiber needs, digestion, or just because they like the texture! You can let your pup eat the grass as long as it is pesticide-free.

## • DOGS LIKE TO BURY THEIR POOP

You might have seen your dog kick up grass after pooping. It seems like the dog is burying its waste, but in reality, it is just marking its territory and communicating with other dogs by scent. By rubbing scent pads (paws), they release [pheromones](https://pets.webmd.com/features/pet-pheromone-products-for-behavior-problems) when they kick at the ground. It signals their territorial claim and sexual availability to other dogs.

## • DOG GNAWS AT BELONGINGS

No, please. Dogs are not maliciously misbehaving. Possibly they're bored. As stated earlier, boredom often results in shredding, pacing, jumping, scratching, and tearing things.
If you want to prevent destructive behavior in your dog, it is best to tire out your puppy with lots of exercise.

## • DOGS THAT GROWL AND SHOUT ARE DANGEROUS

Barking dogs, in general, does not indicate aggression, although some barks may indicate hostility. Typical Chihuahua behaviors include barking when it wakes up, asking for treats, going potty, wanting to cuddle, and so on. Dogs, like humans, can express themselves through sound. Be aware of how your dog barks. While some dogs bark briefly, others growl with deep, guttural sounds. Furthermore, if the hair on your dog's back rises when it barks at a stranger, this is a sign of aggressive behavior.

## • DOGS CAN ALL BE SOCIAL BUTTERFLIES

False! All dogs are not the same. Even with expensive training, some dogs are introverts at heart, just like humans. You shouldn't be hard on yourself if your dog hasn't gotten better socially after spending time at dog parks and attending training sessions. Some dogs just prefer being left alone!

## • OLD DOGS CAN'T LEARN NEW TRICKS

It is a fallacy! With positive reinforcement and a healthy home environment, you absolutely can teach a dog new habits. One of the perks of an older dog is that it has already undergone teething. While older dogs are slower to learn, they can still learn new tricks. Never forget that! There is no age limit to learning! Be sure to reward them.

## • ONLY TIRED DOGS YAWN

Dogs often yawn when anxious or stressed. Often, you will see your dog yawning when they are playing or being stroked by other people, not when they are supposed to be tired. It is also a way for them to express their frustration and lack of patience.

## • DOGS ARE GOOD SWIMMERS

A common misconception is that all dogs are [natural swimmers](/stories/the-cutest-yet-most-practical-dog-accessories-for-all-pet-lovers). While dogs love the water, they aren't all good swimmers.
Bulldogs, mastiffs, dachshunds, and boxers, for example, do not swim well because they have large chests and heavy skulls. Since small dogs are more susceptible to [hyperthermia](https://www.akcchf.org/canine-health/sporting-field-dogs/hyperthermia-in-dogs-fast.html#:~:text=Hyperthermia%2C%20or%20overheating%2C%20according%20to,%C2%B0F%2D102.5%C2%B0F.), they need to go into the water carefully.
